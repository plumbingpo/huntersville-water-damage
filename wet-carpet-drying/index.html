Wet Carpet Drying: What Huntersville Homeowners Should Know

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. In Huntersville, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Warning Signs

  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • A wet spot that keeps growing even though no new water has been added
  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
How We Fix It

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Day 3 to 7

Carpet Starts Failing

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Soaked Pad Underneath

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. The carpet over it and the floor under it keep getting damp from the pad, even once the surface seems dry.

Wet Wall Bottoms

Under the carpet, water travels until it hits the bottom of the walls. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

Mold Under the Carpet

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Furniture Marks

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

We save what can be dried properly and remove only what cannot. Moisture readings make that call, not guesswork.
