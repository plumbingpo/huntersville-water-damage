Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Huntersville

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. Common sources are the roof, an air conditioning drain, a pipe running overhead, or the bathroom one story up. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. We open only what we need to, pull out wet insulation, dry the joists and remaining drywall, then patch, texture and paint so the repair blends in. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Stay out from under it and call.

What It Looks Like

  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
  • Water dripping from a light fixture, vent or smoke detector
  • Discolored rings overhead that get bigger each time it rains or the bathroom is used
  • A musty smell in one room with no visible source
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
  • Hairline cracks spreading outward from a stained or damp spot
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

24 to 48 hours

Stains and Bubbles

You see the first ring, along with blisters in the paint. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Day 3 to 7

Sagging Sets In

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

Past one week

Sections May Fall

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. By now mold has settled into the space above, and the area needing repair has grown considerably.

Our Process

How We Handle Ceiling Water Damage Repair

1

Trace the Leak

The first job is locating the starting point, which involves meter readings, a thermal camera and a look at whatever is over the ceiling. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Make It Safe

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Open and Dry

We take down the ruined drywall and the soaked insulation. Drying equipment, meaning a dehumidifier and air movers, works on the framing and adjacent drywall, with antimicrobial treatment for water that was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

Water follows joists, pipes and ducts, so it can drip several feet from where it entered. We check beyond the visible stain so soaked sections down the line are not overlooked.

Sudden Collapse

Wet drywall is far heavier than dry and loses its grip on the fasteners. An entire piece can fall from a bulging spot onto anything below.

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Some older popcorn textures and plasters contain asbestos. Testing comes first in such a home, ahead of any cutting, scraping or demolition of the damaged section.

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

The structure on the upper side, attic boards or the floor of the next story, tends to be wet too. We check that level as well, because drying only the ceiling leaves moisture behind.
