Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Huntersville

Drywall acts like a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Warning Signs

  • Carpet edges or floorboards wet and discolored right next to the wall
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
  • Discolored lower drywall with a visible line marking how high water wicked
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, wrinkles or peels near the floor
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

How We Fix It

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Cut or Ventilate

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Growth Behind the Paint

Paper lining, darkness and stagnant air make the hidden side of a wall a perfect place for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Insulation Holding Water

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.

Walls You Cannot See

Wet drywall behind cabinets, vanities and built-ins gets no airflow and is easy to overlook. Meter readings at the toe kick and from the far side of the wall reveal it.
